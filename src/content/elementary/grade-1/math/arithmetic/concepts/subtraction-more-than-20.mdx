## Take away the ones, then the tens

Big numbers are made of **tens** and **ones**. When we subtract, we write them in columns, just like adding.

Let's do **47 − 5**. Take away the ones first: 7 − 5 = 2. Then bring down the tens: 4.

Tap **Next step** to see it:

<BorrowSubtraction a={47} b={5} />

So 47 − 5 = **42**.

<Callout type="tip" title="Hoot's trick">
  Always start with the **ones**. Then do the tens.
</Callout>

## Not enough ones? Borrow!

Now let's do **42 − 7**. Look at the ones: we have 2 ones, but we need to take away 7. There aren't enough!

So we **borrow**: break 1 ten into 10 ones.

<TensOnes n={42} />

42 is 4 tens and 2 ones. After breaking a ten, it's **3 tens and 12 ones**. Now 12 − 7 = 5, and we have 3 tens left.

<BorrowSubtraction a={42} b={7} />

So 42 − 7 = **35**.

<Callout type="remember" title="Remember">
  1 ten = 10 ones. When there aren't enough ones, cross out the tens digit, make it 1 less, and put 10 more with the ones!
</Callout>

## Two big numbers

It works the same way with two big numbers. Let's do **52 − 28**.

1. Ones: 2 − 8? Not enough! Borrow: the 5 tens become **4**, and the 2 ones become **12**.
2. Ones: 12 − 8 = **4**.
3. Tens: 4 − 2 = **2**.

<BorrowSubtraction a={52} b={28} />

So 52 − 28 = **24**!
